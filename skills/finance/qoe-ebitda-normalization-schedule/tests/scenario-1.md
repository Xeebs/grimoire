# Scenario 1: Specialty Distribution Company — Buy-Side QofE, Contested Add-Backs and PPP Direction

## Context

An M&A analyst at a regional investment bank is performing buy-side quality of earnings on behalf of a strategic acquirer evaluating the acquisition of Midwest Industrial Supply Co., a specialty distributor of MRO (maintenance, repair, and operations) supplies serving manufacturing customers in the upper Midwest. The deal is at LOI stage; the acquirer's lender has requested a QofE-supported adjusted EBITDA before finalizing senior debt sizing.

The target is an owner-operated business with $45M in revenue. Management has submitted their own QofE package with proposed add-backs. The analyst's task is to independently evaluate management's proposed adjustments and produce a defensibility-rated schedule. The deal team needs to know: what is the defensible range of adjusted EBITDA, and which items are likely to draw pushback from the sell-side QofE firm or the lender's credit team?

### FLAGGED ITEMS FOR QofE REVIEW

1. **Owner compensation above market rate**
   - Period(s): FY2021, FY2022, FY2023
   - Amount: $850K/year (excess over estimated $500K market rate)
   - Management's proposed treatment: Non-Recurring Add-Back
   - Notes: No formal compensation study has been prepared

2. **ERP implementation costs**
   - Period(s): FY2023 only
   - Amount: $420K
   - Management's proposed treatment: Non-Recurring Add-Back
   - Notes: System went live in Q4 2023; implementation vendor invoices available

3. **PPP loan forgiveness income**
   - Period(s): FY2021 only
   - Amount: $180K
   - Management's proposed treatment: Not proposed (presented as neutral)
   - Notes: Federal Paycheck Protection Program forgiveness; recognized as other income in FY2021

4. **Litigation settlement — paid**
   - Period(s): FY2023 only
   - Amount: $310K
   - Management's proposed treatment: Non-Recurring Add-Back
   - Notes: Final judgment entered June 2023; counsel confirms no related matters pending

5. **Owner auto & travel — personal component**
   - Period(s): FY2021, FY2022, FY2023
   - Amount: $95K/year
   - Management's proposed treatment: Non-Recurring Add-Back
   - Notes: No allocation schedule prepared; owner has not provided a signed representation letter; the full $95K line item is in G&A under "Auto & Travel — Owner"

6. **"Strategic consulting" fees**
   - Period(s): FY2021, FY2022, FY2023
   - Amount: $230K/year
   - Management's proposed treatment: Non-Recurring Add-Back
   - Notes: Same dollar amount in each of the three years reviewed; management describes these as costs related to a market expansion initiative

## Expected Output Criteria

- [ ] Section A correctly classifies the ERP implementation ($420K, FY2023 only) as a Non-Recurring Add-Back rated Conservative, with rationale referencing one-time nature and available vendor documentation
- [ ] Section A correctly classifies the litigation settlement ($310K, FY2023) as a Non-Recurring Add-Back rated Conservative, with rationale referencing final judgment and no pending claims
- [ ] Section A correctly classifies PPP loan forgiveness income ($180K, FY2021) as a Non-Recurring DEDUCTION — not a neutral item and not an add-back — with rationale explaining that this income inflated reported FY2021 EBITDA and a buyer would not expect it to recur post-close
- [ ] Section A correctly classifies owner compensation excess ($850K/year) as a Non-Recurring Add-Back rated Moderate, with rationale noting the absence of a formal comp study as the basis for Moderate (not Conservative) defensibility
- [ ] Section A correctly classifies "strategic consulting" fees ($230K/year) as RECURRING — citing the identical dollar amount appearing in all three years as evidence of recurrence regardless of management's characterization — and assigns no adjustment
- [ ] Section A correctly classifies owner auto & travel ($95K/year) as REQUIRES CONFIRMATION, citing the absence of an allocation schedule or owner representation letter, and specifies the exact management deliverable needed (a signed expense allocation schedule or personal use representation)
- [ ] Section B shows the EBITDA bridge for FY2021, FY2022, and FY2023, each starting from Reported EBITDA as a separately stated line
- [ ] Section B correctly shows the PPP deduction as a negative (parenthetical) amount reducing FY2021 Adjusted EBITDA, not as a zero or neutral line
- [ ] Section B correctly excludes the "strategic consulting" fees from the bridge (classified as Recurring — no adjustment)
- [ ] Section B correctly excludes the auto & travel item from the confirmed adjusted EBITDA bridge and shows it only in the "Items Pending Confirmation" line
- [ ] Section C provides both a Conservative case and a High case for the defensible LTM Adjusted EBITDA range (LTM should reference FY2023 as the most recent full year in the absence of partial-year data)
- [ ] Section C flags that management's proposed treatment of PPP income as neutral is incorrect — the schedule must correct this regardless of whether management agreed
- [ ] Section C does not trigger the 15% Aggressive concentration warning, since no items are classified Aggressive (they are Moderate, Conservative, REQUIRES CONFIRMATION, or Recurring)

## What failure looks like

A failing output would:
- Treat the PPP loan forgiveness income as a neutral pass-through rather than deducting it from FY2021 EBITDA (a direction error that overstates the normalized FY2021 figure)
- Accept management's characterization of "strategic consulting" as non-recurring without noting the three-year recurrence pattern
- Classify the owner auto & travel adjustment as a confirmed Non-Recurring Add-Back (instead of REQUIRES CONFIRMATION) because the dollar amount is small
- Include the auto & travel item in the confirmed adjusted EBITDA bridge without flagging it
- Rate the owner compensation add-back as Conservative rather than Moderate due to the absence of a comp study
- Fail to produce a period-by-period bridge and instead report only a single LTM adjusted EBITDA figure
- Omit a rationale for any adjustment, relying instead on accounting labels alone
