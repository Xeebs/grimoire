# Scenario 2: Specialty Industrial Comps — Distressed Outlier and M&A Premium Outlier

## Context

An investment banking analyst at a middle-market advisory firm is preparing the comparable companies section of a buy-side pitchbook for a strategic acquirer evaluating Meridian Precision Components, a specialty manufacturer of machined components for aerospace and defense OEMs. The comps table has been built using public industrials data. The analyst must produce the written commentary accompanying the table before the client presentation in two days.

## Input

### Comparable Companies Table

| Company | EV ($M) | EV/LTM EBITDA | EV/NTM EBITDA | P/E LTM | P/E NTM | LTM Rev Growth | LTM EBITDA Margin | LTM Rev ($M) |
|---------|---------|----------------|----------------|---------|---------|----------------|-------------------|--------------|
| TransDigm Group | 24,100 | 22.4x | 20.1x | 38.6x | 33.2x | 12% | 46% | 7,200 |
| Heico Corporation | 18,600 | 26.8x | 24.2x | 44.1x | 40.3x | 18% | 24% | 3,800 |
| Ducommun Incorporated | 980 | 8.3x | 7.6x | 14.2x | 12.8x | 6% | 10% | 760 |
| Moog Inc. | 3,400 | 10.9x | 10.1x | 16.8x | 15.4x | 9% | 14% | 3,200 |
| Kaman Corporation | 1,100 | 9.2x | 8.7x | 15.1x | 13.9x | (2%) | 11% | 680 |
| CPI Aerostructures | 210 | 5.1x | 4.8x | NM | NM | (8%) | 7% | 195 |

**Median**: EV/NTM EBITDA 9.4x; P/E NTM 14.7x

### Target Company Description

**Meridian Precision Components** is a specialty manufacturer of close-tolerance machined titanium and aluminum components for aerospace and defense prime contractors and Tier 1 OEMs. Key financial characteristics:

- LTM Revenue: $310M
- NTM Revenue estimate: $340M
- LTM EBITDA: $52M
- LTM EBITDA Margin: 16.8% — above peer median
- NTM EBITDA estimate: $58M
- LTM Revenue growth: 7%
- Customer concentration: Top 3 customers represent 58% of revenue (Boeing 24%, Lockheed Martin 19%, Raytheon 15%)
- Contract backlog: $480M (1.5x LTM revenue coverage)
- Revenue mix: 78% defense, 22% commercial aerospace

### Additional Context

Heico Corporation received a strategic acquisition inquiry from a larger defense prime in Q1 of this fiscal year; the inquiry was disclosed in an 8-K but Heico's board has not confirmed a formal sale process. CPI Aerostructures has been operating under a financial restructuring plan following a DOD contract dispute that resulted in an $18M charge in the prior fiscal year. Kaman Corporation recently completed a divestiture of its distribution segment, shifting to a pure-play aerospace profile.

## Expected Output Criteria

- [ ] Section B identifies Heico Corporation as a premium outlier (26.8x LTM EBITDA vs. 9.4x NTM median) and cites the disclosed acquisition inquiry from a defense prime as the proposed rationale — not a generic "M&A speculation" label
- [ ] Section B identifies CPI Aerostructures as a discount outlier (5.1x NTM EBITDA, below median and trading at NM P/E) and cites the DOD contract dispute and financial restructuring plan as the proposed rationale
- [ ] Section A includes a one-sentence inclusion rationale for at least four of the six peers referencing a specific business characteristic (e.g., aerospace/defense revenue mix, machined components, Tier 1 customer base, margin profile)
- [ ] Section C references Meridian's 16.8% EBITDA margin (above peer median of approximately 14%) as a factor supporting a premium to median, and references the 58% customer concentration in the top 3 customers as a factor that may limit premium vs. diversified peers — both present in the same positioning argument
- [ ] Section C states a specific implied enterprise value range in dollars using at least one named multiple range applied to Meridian's $58M NTM EBITDA estimate
- [ ] Section D contains exactly 3–5 callout bullets, each a single sentence in present tense with at least one specific number; at least one callout explicitly addresses the dual-outlier structure (one at premium, one at discount) rather than treating the peer set as uniform

## What failure looks like

A failing output would: identify Heico as an outlier based on general "M&A premium" language without referencing the disclosed acquisition inquiry; identify CPI Aerostructures as merely "low-growth" without acknowledging the restructuring context; produce a target positioning argument that ignores customer concentration risk; write a Section C that applies multiples to LTM rather than NTM EBITDA when both are available; or produce slide callouts that describe the peer set in general terms without referencing the specific outlier dynamic (one premium, one distressed) that defines this comps set.
