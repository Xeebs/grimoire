# Scenario 1: Industrials Carve-Out — Precision Parts Manufacturer

## Context

A mid-market PE associate at a firm with $3B AUM is reviewing a first-pass LBO model generated by an AI tool from the CIM for Hartwell Precision Components, a carve-out of a precision-machined parts division from a diversified industrial conglomerate. The deal team has been running the AI tool internally for six months; this is the fourth deal where it has produced a model. The associate needs to sign off before the model goes to the Investment Committee next week. The firm has specific modeling conventions that differ from the AI tool's defaults.

The AI tool exported a model summary that the associate will paste into this skill. The firm's convention list has been maintained in a shared drive and the associate copies it in verbatim.

## Input

**DEAL OVERVIEW**
Target company: Hartwell Precision Components (carve-out from Hartwell Industries, Inc.)
Sector: Industrials / Manufacturing
Brief business description: Precision-machined aerospace and defense components manufacturer; ~85% revenue from long-term OEM contracts with two major defense primes; remainder from commercial MRO.

Entry Assumptions:
- LTM EBITDA at close: $152.0M
- Entry multiple: 8.5x EV/EBITDA
- Enterprise value: $1,292.0M
- Total equity contribution: $387.6M (30% of EV)
- Net debt at close: $904.4M
- Management equity rollover: 5% of equity value (~$19.4M)

Debt Structure:
- Term Loan A (TLA): $380.0M, L+250 bps (floating), 5% annual mandatory amortization, 6-year maturity
- Term Loan B (TLB): $380.0M, L+325 bps (floating), 1% annual mandatory amortization, 7-year maturity

Operating Projections (Year 1 through Year 5):
- Revenue Year 0 (LTM): $760.0M; Year 1: $784.8M; Year 2: $808.6M; Year 3: $833.5M; Year 4: $859.6M; Year 5: $886.6M

Model notes from AI tool:
- Revolver assumed drawn to $0 at close and held flat at $0 through hold period (no seasonal draw modeled)
- EBITDA used in leverage covenant test is as-reported EBITDA (no add-backs modeled)
- IRR calculated on total enterprise cash flows; labeled as "sponsor IRR" in model output
- All interest calculated on beginning-of-period debt balance

**FIRM MODELING CONVENTIONS**

1. All monetary values displayed in $M with two decimal places
2. Hard-coded input cells formatted in blue (Pantone 2144 C); formulas in black
3. No hardcoded constants embedded in formula cells — all inputs must reference a dedicated Assumptions tab
4. Debt schedule uses a separate tab per tranche with a full corkscrew balance schedule
5. Cash sweep must be applied in order: TLA first, then TLB, then revolver — per our standard credit agreement template
6. IRR and MOIC reported on both gross basis and net of (a) transaction fees (assumed 2% of EV) and (b) management promote (per deal-specific waterfall)
7. Sensitivity tables required on Summary tab: 3x3 grid for entry multiple vs. revenue CAGR, and 3x3 grid for exit multiple vs. EBITDA margin
8. A Sources and Uses tab must be present with all line items citing the relevant CIM section
9. Version control notation on cover tab: version number, date, associate initials
10. SOFR curve must be modeled using the forward curve from Bloomberg as of deal date — flat rate assumption not acceptable

## Expected Output Criteria

- [ ] Section 1 identifies all five debt tranches by name with their stated amounts, rates, and amortization terms; flags missing net MOIC / net IRR as [DATA GAP]
- [ ] Section 2 flags the IRR calculation as FAIL because the model computes it on enterprise cash flows rather than equity cash flows
- [ ] Section 2 flags the cash sweep tranche priority as FAIL because the AI model applies sweep to TLB first while the firm convention requires TLA first
- [ ] Section 2 flags the revolver edge case as NEEDS VERIFICATION or FAIL, noting that a $0 revolver balance held flat through the hold period with no seasonal draw creates unrealistic FCF timing for a manufacturing business
- [ ] Section 3 flags capex at 1.6% of revenue as MATERIAL deviation below the industrials benchmark of 3–7%
- [ ] Section 3 confirms revenue CAGR of 3.1% is Within Range for industrials
- [ ] Section 3 confirms total leverage at 5.95x as MATERIAL above the industrials benchmark of 4–5.5x
- [ ] Section 4 generates a stress-test matrix with all seven named scenarios, including a Combined Downside and a Multiple Contraction scenario; at least one scenario must show IRR below the 20% hurdle
- [ ] Section 5 marks Convention #5 (cash sweep order) as FAIL with a specific finding referencing TLA-first requirement
- [ ] Section 5 marks Convention #6 (net MOIC/IRR) as FAIL because net figures are not modeled
- [ ] Section 5 marks Convention #10 (SOFR forward curve) as FAIL because the model uses a flat 5.25% assumption
- [ ] Executive Summary overall status is NEEDS REVISION or SIGNIFICANT REWORK REQUIRED (not READY FOR PRESENTATION)
- [ ] Executive Summary Top 3 priority actions reference the IRR calculation error, the cash sweep priority error, and at least one of the convention failures — not generic observations
- [ ] Section 6 lists the missing net returns and flat SOFR rate as data gaps requiring resolution before IC submission

## What failure looks like

A failing output would:
- Produce a narrative paragraph review instead of the required structured tables
- Miss the IRR calculation error (enterprise vs. equity cash flows) — this is the most consequential formula error in the input
- Describe the capex deviation as "slightly below typical" without flagging it as MATERIAL using the benchmark delta methodology
- Generate a stress-test section with only one or two scenarios instead of all seven named scenarios
- Mark Convention #5 (cash sweep order) as PASS because TLB is a valid prepayment target — missing that the firm convention specifies TLA-first
- Produce an Executive Summary that says READY FOR PRESENTATION despite three FAIL items in Section 2 alone
- Include language like "this appears to be a solid model" or "the returns look attractive" — qualitative investment judgments that the skill explicitly prohibits
