# Scenario 1: SaaS Company Revenue Raise with Emerging Macro Hedge

## Context

Maya Okonkwo is a mid-cap software equity research analyst at a regional investment bank covering Velorix Inc. (ticker: VLRX), a cloud-based field service management SaaS company with ~$380M trailing revenue. She has just downloaded the Q4 2024 earnings call transcript (released this morning) and has the Q3 2024 transcript from last quarter in her files. Her immediate task: update her forward estimates and determine whether the $500M FY2025 revenue target management has been telegraphing all year remains intact, and whether last quarter's unusually confident tone has held. She needs an analyst brief to accompany her model update before the 11am team standup.

## Input

I'm proud of what the team delivered.

But if the SMB softening we're seeing extends into Q2, we'd be operating closer to the lower end of that range.

On operating margins, David will cover this in detail, but I want to flag upfront that we're investing heavily in go-to-market in the first half of the year. We expect some margin pressure in H1 before we get the leverage benefits in H2. We're targeting non-GAAP operating margins in the range of 14 to 16 percent for the full year, roughly in line with 2024 levels, though I'd say we're at the more comfortable end of that range only if the revenue trajectory holds.

**David Fehn (CFO)**: Thank you, Priya. I'll take you through the financials and then provide additional color on guidance. Full-year 2024 revenue came in at $381.6 million, up 24% year-over-year. Q4 revenue of $101.2 million was up 22% year-over-year.

International expansion — we continue to invest in our EMEA go-to-market motion. We are on track to launch our German and French localization in Q2, which we believe will meaningfully expand our addressable market. We're not providing revenue contribution guidance for international at this stage, but we're optimistic about the long-term opportunity.

**Priya Ashworth (CEO)**: Alex, I think that's a fair characterization of how we're thinking about it, yes. The range is designed to accommodate both scenarios. We'll have more clarity by the Q1 call.

**Alex Torres (Meridian Securities)**: Great. And the 14 to 16 percent non-GAAP operating margin — is that still the right range even in the downside revenue scenario?

**David Fehn (CFO)**: In the downside scenario, that range would likely compress. We have some fixed cost structure in go-to-market that doesn't flex quickly. **Operator**: Our next question comes from Simone Park from Westfield Capital.

**Simone Park (Westfield Capital)**: Thanks. David, you mentioned FCF is back-half weighted — just so I'm modeling correctly, are you still thinking something like 15 to 18 percent FCF margin for the full year? That's what you indicated at your investor day.

**Simone Park (Westfield Capital)**: So no change to the investor day target then?

**David Fehn (CFO)**: We're not reaffirming or changing any specific figure today. I'd focus on our qualitative commentary for now, and we'll give you more precision on Q1.
