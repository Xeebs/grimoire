# Scenario 2: Healthcare Services Company — New Money Facility with Revenue Growth but Deteriorating Margins and Concentration Risk

## Context

A credit analyst at a mid-size commercial bank is preparing the initial credit approval memo for a new relationship with Clearpath Home Health Services, Inc., a Medicare and Medicaid-certified home health agency headquartered in Nashville, Tennessee. Clearpath is requesting a $4.0 million revolving credit facility and a $3.5 million term loan to fund working capital and the acquisition of a smaller regional competitor. The company is majority-owned by two co-founders who are also active operators; a minority private equity sponsor (Greenfield Health Partners) holds a 25% stake and has a board seat.

The credit officer has provided the analyst with a financial spread for FY2022–FY2024, a term sheet, a business description, and a one-paragraph industry context note. This is a first-time credit relationship for the bank. The credit profile has mixed signals: revenue has grown rapidly but EBITDA margins have compressed materially, and the company's revenue is heavily concentrated in government payor programs subject to reimbursement rate changes.

### INDUSTRY CONTEXT (provided by relationship officer)
Home health is a high-growth segment of the broader post-acute care market, driven by aging demographics and CMS's stated preference for lower-cost home-based settings over facility care. The Medicare home health PPS reimbursement rates have faced pressure from behavioral adjustment (PDGM) reforms implemented since 2020, which reduced payments for high-utilization episodes. Labor costs — primarily clinical staff wages — represent the largest cost driver and have increased materially across the sector since 2021 due to a nationwide shortage of home health aides and skilled nurses. Medicaid reimbursement rates vary significantly by state and are subject to legislative risk. Companies operating in multiple states face regulatory compliance complexity with respect to licensure and certificate of need (CON) requirements.

## What failure looks like

A failing output would:
- Characterize the revenue growth as uniformly positive without flagging the concurrent EBITDA margin compression — treating growth and margin deterioration as independent observations rather than identifying the divergence as the core analytical issue
- Omit the government payor concentration risk or treat it as a LOW severity item despite the borrower's stated revenue mix being predominantly Medicare and Medicaid
- Present the pro forma Debt/EBITDA and DSCR figures as confirmed post-acquisition metrics rather than preliminary estimates based on unaudited seller P&L
- Cite market statistics not present in the supplied industry context paragraph (e.g., a specific national market size figure for home health, a specific labor shortage percentage) — presenting training-data knowledge as sourced industry analysis
- Assign a FULL offset assessment to government payor concentration risk based solely on the borrowing base structure, without acknowledging that reimbursement rate risk is not mitigated by collateral
- Fail to flag the Valley Home Care QofE condition in the risks or recommendation section
