# Scenario 2: Critical-Tier Account with Imminent Renewal, Active Competitive Threat, and Champion Turnover

## Context

The CSM is Daniel Okafor, a strategic accounts lead at a B2B SaaS contract lifecycle management (CLM) platform. His account, Fortbridge Manufacturing (a mid-size industrial manufacturer, 1,200 employees, 4 plants across the US), has been a customer for 38 months on a $195,000 ACV contract covering 250 seats and three product modules (Contract Authoring, Obligation Tracking, and Supplier Portal). The renewal date is August 1, 2026 — 47 days out.

The account has entered a crisis window: the champion who drove the original purchase (VP of Legal, Carla Mendez) was laid off 54 days ago as part of a 12% company-wide workforce reduction. Her replacement has not been named. The new interim contact is the General Counsel, Robert Fitch, who did not sponsor the original purchase and has indicated he is reviewing all contracts above $100K as part of his mandate to reduce legal tech spend. A competitor (IronClad) was mentioned by Robert in his first introductory call with Daniel 22 days ago. The renewal has not been formally initiated and only 47 days remain. Product usage has declined since Carla's departure. Daniel is doing emergency renewal triage and needs a clear action brief he can bring to his VP of CS today.

## Input

**Account**: Fortbridge Manufacturing
**ACV**: $195,000
**Renewal Date**: 2026-08-01
**Days Until Renewal**: 47

**Adoption Depth**
- DAU/MAU ratio: 17% (down from 38% six months ago; decline began approximately 6 weeks ago, correlating with Carla Mendez's departure)
- License utilization: 89 of 250 seats active in past 30 days (36%; was 67% six months ago)
- Key features active: Contract Authoring (active, used by 22 of 89 active users); Obligation Tracking (partially active — set up but alerts have been disabled by an admin; unclear if intentional); Supplier Portal (active, used by plant operations teams; appears to be running independently of the legal team's engagement)
- Days since last active user login: 0 days (Supplier Portal activity from plant team today; 8 days since any legal team user last logged in)

**Relationship Health**
- Champion: Vacant — Carla Mendez (former VP of Legal) was laid off 54 days ago; her replacement has not been named; interim contact is Robert Fitch (General Counsel), first contacted by Daniel 22 days ago (introductory call, 45 minutes)
- Executive sponsor: Robert Fitch (General Counsel) is now also the de facto executive sponsor (no senior champion beneath him to manage the relationship); no VP of CS or executive has yet contacted Robert at peer level
- Open escalation tickets: 0 open escalations
- Support ticket trend (past 90 days): Decreasing — 8 tickets in Q4 2025, 3 tickets in Q1 2026, 1 ticket so far in Q2 2026; decline correlates with reduced usage, not resolution of issues

**Value Realization**
- ROI documentation: None — Carla Mendez had informally tracked contract cycle time improvements but no formal ROI document was ever produced; her departure took all institutional knowledge of usage outcomes with her
- Last QBR/EBR date: October 2025 (9 months ago); a Q1 2026 EBR was scheduled with Carla but never rescheduled after her departure
- Business outcomes delivered vs. promised: Three outcomes were committed at sale — (1) reduce average contract cycle time from 18 days to under 7 days (not measured; no data available); (2) achieve 100% supplier contract digitization across 4 plants (Supplier Portal is running at 3 of 4 plants — Plant 4 not yet onboarded after 38 months); (3) eliminate manual obligation tracking (obligation tracking alerts disabled; current status unknown)
- Expansion conversation status: Never held; Carla had discussed expanding to the HR team for employment contracts in an informal email 6 months ago, but no formal conversation was held and no current contact is pursuing this

**Competitive Exposure**
- Renewal conversation initiated: No; Daniel asked about renewal on the May 13 introductory call and Robert said "I need to review all our legal tech contracts before I can commit to anything — I'll come back to you in a few weeks." No follow-up meeting has been booked.
- Competitor evaluation signals: Robert Fitch mentioned IronClad by name on the May 13 call, saying "we've been approached by IronClad and they're offering a significant discount on a new implementation" — exact discount not stated but Robert described it as "meaningful"
- Budget constraint signals: Robert explicitly stated he has a mandate to reduce legal tech spend by 20% in FY2027; the $195K ACV exceeds his unilateral sign-off threshold; CFO approval will be required for renewal
- Stakeholder changes in past 90 days: 2 — Carla Mendez (VP Legal, primary champion) laid off 54 days ago; a plant operations VP (James Holloway, Supplier Portal sponsor) was also promoted to COO 30 days ago, which may represent a new executive stakeholder

**Additional context**: Daniel has one prior relationship in the account beyond Carla — Lisa Park (Senior Contracts Manager), who has been using Contract Authoring daily and is broadly supportive of the platform but has no authority over the renewal decision and is not on Robert Fitch's direct team. Obligation Tracking alerts being disabled is a known risk: without alerts, Fortbridge may already be missing contract obligation milestones, which would create a business impact event that could go either way — it validates the platform's necessity or becomes a blame point if something goes wrong. The new COO (James Holloway) was the internal champion for the Supplier Portal implementation and has direct experience with the platform's value in plant operations.

## Expected Output Criteria

- [ ] Adoption Depth scored High Risk — DAU/MAU of 17% is below the 20% threshold for High Risk; license utilization of 36% is below the 40% threshold for High Risk; both criteria independently trigger High Risk; output must score this dimension High Risk
- [ ] Relationship Health scored High Risk — champion role is vacant (54 days since last champion contact exceeds the 45-day High Risk threshold); Robert Fitch, now the de facto executive sponsor, was first contacted only 22 days ago (not a replacement for a well-established champion); output must treat the vacant champion as a High Risk signal, not as a Medium Risk one
- [ ] Value Realization scored High Risk — no ROI documentation exists, last QBR/EBR was 9 months ago (> 180 days = High Risk trigger), fewer than 50% of promised outcomes are documented as delivered (Supplier Portal 3 of 4 plants, cycle time unmeasured, obligation tracking status unknown), and no expansion conversation has been held
- [ ] Competitive Exposure scored High Risk — renewal not initiated, IronClad named competitor with a stated discount offer, 20% budget reduction mandate confirmed, 2 stakeholder changes in 90 days; all High Risk sub-criteria are present simultaneously
- [ ] All four dimensions scored High Risk — this is one of the most severe risk profiles the scoring model can produce
- [ ] Tier classified as CRITICAL — Executive Save Play (High-Value), triggered by two or more dimensions being High Risk; output must explicitly state this classification rule
- [ ] Output acknowledges that with only 47 days until renewal, an Adoption Acceleration Sprint is not a viable primary intervention and must not be recommended (the constraint against this action < 60 days is explicit in the skill)
- [ ] Interventions selected must include Executive Sponsor Reconnect (VP/CSO to Robert Fitch, given the lapsed executive relationship and competitive threat), Renewal Conversation Initiation (47 days out, no formal renewal opened), and either Competitive Differentiation Brief (IronClad named) or Stakeholder Mapping and Influence Assessment (COO James Holloway as new potential executive champion) — selection of any two plus a third defensible choice is acceptable
- [ ] The COO (James Holloway) appears explicitly in the action brief as a new executive stakeholder to engage — his promotion and his prior role as Supplier Portal champion make him a high-leverage contact that should not be missed
- [ ] Talking points for Robert Fitch address his specific context: the 20% spend reduction mandate, the IronClad alternative, and the need for CFO approval — talking points must not be operational (feature usage metrics) but executive-level (business risk of switching, implementation cost of IronClad migration, Supplier Portal's measured value at the plant level)
- [ ] The disabled Obligation Tracking alerts are explicitly called out in the brief as a risk to surface with Robert Fitch — either as a business risk that validates the platform's necessity or as a known gap that should be addressed before the renewal conversation
- [ ] Data Gaps section identifies: (1) the IronClad discount amount is unstated (affects competitive response strategy); (2) CFO identity and prior relationship with the platform is unknown; (3) contract cycle time improvement has never been measured (weakens the renewal value story); (4) Obligation Tracking alert status is unclear (disabled by admin — intentional or oversight unknown)

## What failure looks like

- Scoring any of the four dimensions as Medium or Low Risk — all four have explicit High Risk triggers that are present in the input data; any sub-High scoring on this account represents a scoring error
- Classifying the account as AT-RISK — QBR Acceleration — the account has two or more High Risk dimensions plus an ACV well above the $50K threshold; CRITICAL is the only correct classification
- Recommending Adoption Acceleration Sprint as an intervention — the renewal is 47 days out, which is inside the 60-day constraint; this would be a direct violation of the skill's guardrails
- Failing to surface James Holloway (COO, former Supplier Portal champion) as a high-leverage executive contact — his existence in the input is a key differentiating signal that a surface-level read of the account would miss
- Producing talking points for Robert Fitch that focus on feature utilization rates or adoption metrics — Robert's decision lens is spend reduction and competitive alternatives, not operational platform health; feature-focused talking points are mismatched to this executive's context
- Missing the disabled Obligation Tracking alerts as a risk signal — this is a latent business impact event that could become a negative proof point against the platform if not surfaced proactively
- Treating the decreasing support ticket trend as a positive signal without noting that it correlates with declining usage, not with issue resolution — a naive reading of "tickets declining = good" misses the actual signal
