# Scenario 1: Personal Injury — Multi-Witness, Phone Use and Turning Direction

## Context

A litigation associate at a plaintiff's personal injury firm is preparing cross-examination outlines three weeks before trial in *Hargrove v. Webb*, a vehicular collision case. The defendant driver, Marcus Webb, and an eyewitness pedestrian, Carol Finch, have each been deposed. Webb's deposition ran a full day; Finch's was a half-day session. The associate also has Webb's voluntary statement to the responding police officer, which Webb signed at the scene. The central disputed facts are: (1) whether Webb was distracted by his phone at or before the collision, and (2) whether Webb had the legal right-of-way when the collision occurred. The associate needs a prioritized list of impeachment points before drafting cross-examination outlines.

## Input

---

### CASE SUMMARY

*Hargrove v. Webb*, Case No. 24-CV-07841 (Superior Court). Plaintiff Diana Hargrove alleges defendant Marcus Webb struck her vehicle while she was lawfully proceeding through the intersection of Granger Ave. and Elm St. on March 14, 2024. Webb claims Hargrove ran a red light. Key disputed facts: (1) which driver had the right-of-way (traffic signal phase), (2) whether Webb was distracted by his phone immediately before impact, and (3) the direction of Webb's turn onto Granger Ave., which bears on the signal phase analysis.

### DEPOSITION OF MARCUS WEBB, APRIL 22, 2025 (Excerpts)

**MORNING SESSION — Pages 40–120**

Webb Dep. 44:2–10
Q: Mr. Webb, at the moment you entered the intersection, which direction were you intending to turn?
A: I was making a left turn onto Granger. I'd been in the left-turn lane for maybe half a block.

Webb Dep. 44:11–15
Q: And you were traveling southbound on Elm before that turn?
A: That's right. Southbound, then left onto Granger heading east.

Webb Dep. 47:8–18
Q: Describe what you were doing in the thirty seconds before the collision.
A: I was watching the signal, waiting for the green arrow. When it came on I started to move. I was making a left turn onto Granger when I heard the impact — it was immediate.

Webb Dep. 62:3–9
Q: Mr. Webb, did you use your phone at any point during your drive that morning before the accident?
A: No. I didn't use my phone at all.

Webb Dep. 62:10–14
Q: Not to call, not to text, not to navigate?
A: No. Nothing like that. My phone was in the cupholder the whole time.

Webb Dep. 78:20–79:4
Q: How long had you been driving before the accident occurred?
A: Maybe twenty-five minutes. I'd left my apartment around eight and it happened around eight twenty-five.

Webb Dep. 80:5–12
Q: Was there any point during that drive where you picked up your phone?
A: No. I just told you. My phone was in the cupholder. I don't touch my phone when I'm driving — that's a rule I have for myself.

**AFTERNOON SESSION — Pages 100–165**

Webb Dep. 93:1–8
Q: I want to return to the morning of the accident. You testified this morning that you did not use your phone at any point during the drive. I'm going to ask you to look at this screenshot of your Google Maps app, Exhibit 14. Does this refresh your recollection about whether you used Maps that morning?
A: I — yeah, okay. I checked a map briefly, maybe five minutes before — just a quick glance at the directions. I forgot about that. It was so quick.

Webb Dep. 93:9–14
Q: So you did use your phone approximately five minutes before the collision?
A: I looked at the map for a second. I wouldn't call that "using" my phone. It was a glance.

Webb Dep. 110:3–9
Q: I'm handing you what's been marked as Exhibit 16, a diagram of the Granger and Elm intersection. Using this diagram, can you mark the lane you were in and the direction of your intended turn?
A: [pause] Okay, I'm marking it.

Webb Dep. 114:4–12
Q: And for the record, you've drawn an arrow from the northbound approach on Elm, turning right. Is that correct?
A: Looking at the diagram — yes, I was going right. Turning right onto Granger.

Webb Dep. 114:13–19
Q: Mr. Webb, you testified this morning that you were in the left-turn lane heading southbound on Elm and making a left turn onto Granger heading east. Now you're telling me you were making a right turn from the northbound approach?
A: The diagram is confusing me. I think I had the directions switched in my head this morning.

Webb Dep. 115:1–6
Q: Which answer is correct?
A: I'm honestly not sure. The diagram is oriented differently than how I picture the intersection.

### DEPOSITION OF CAROL FINCH, APRIL 29, 2025 (Excerpts)

Finch Dep. 18:4–11
Q: Ms. Finch, where were you standing when the collision occurred?
A: I was on the northwest corner of Granger and Elm, waiting to cross. I had a clear view of the intersection — I'd been standing there maybe forty seconds waiting for the walk signal.

Finch Dep. 24:6–16
Q: Could you see the defendant's vehicle before impact?
A: Yes. He came through — I could see him through the windshield. His head was down. He was looking at something in the center console area, not at the road.

Finch Dep. 24:17–22
Q: Did you see a phone in his hand or visible anywhere?
A: I could see the glow of a screen. Something lit up in the cupholder area or on the console. His eyes were on it, not on the signal.

Finch Dep. 31:3–9
Q: For how long did you observe his head being down?
A: It wasn't a quick thing. I noticed it when he was maybe two or three car lengths back from the stop line. That's when I thought, oh, that's not good.

Finch Dep. 44:19–45:3
Q: Ms. Finch, I want to ask you about Mr. Webb's phone specifically. Did you see a phone in his hand?
A: I didn't see it in his hand. I saw the glow — the screen lit up. Whatever it was, it was in the console area and he was looking at it.

### PRIOR SWORN STATEMENT — WEBB VOLUNTARY POLICE STATEMENT, MARCH 14, 2024

Transcribed from officer body camera footage. Signed by Marcus Webb at scene, 9:07 a.m., March 14, 2024.

"I want to cooperate fully. I was driving to work, completely focused on the road. I had not used my phone in the ten minutes prior to the collision — I know that for a fact because I have a strict rule about it. My phone was in the cupholder untouched. The other driver ran the red light and I had no time to brake."

## Expected Output Criteria

- [ ] The brief header correctly lists both Webb and Finch as witnesses analyzed and identifies the Webb Police Statement as the prior sworn statement analyzed
- [ ] The brief correctly identifies and classifies a PRIOR SWORN contradiction between Webb's deposition admission (Dep. 93:1–9) and the police statement, placed at Tier 1, with verbatim quotes from both documents and citations to both
- [ ] The brief correctly identifies and classifies an INTERNAL contradiction between Webb's morning testimony about turning left (Dep. 47:8–18 or Dep. 44:2–10) and his afternoon testimony about turning right (Dep. 114:4–12), placed at Tier 2 (not Tier 1, because Webb himself acknowledged the diagram confused him, making it susceptible to explanation)
- [ ] The brief correctly identifies and classifies a CROSS-WITNESS contradiction between Webb's claim that his phone was in the cupholder and untouched (Dep. 62:3–14 or Dep. 80:5–12) and Finch's observation of a lit screen in the console area with Webb's eyes on it (Finch Dep. 24:6–22), with appropriate tier assignment (Tier 1 or Tier 2 are both defensible — the criterion is that a tier assignment is made and a cross-examination note explains the deployment)
- [ ] All Statement A and Statement B fields contain verbatim quoted text from the input, not paraphrase
- [ ] All citations reference the specific page:line numbers provided in the input (e.g., "Webb Dep. 93:1–8" not vague references to "afternoon testimony")
- [ ] The cross-examination note for the Tier 1 prior sworn item describes the sequencing tactic (lock in the denial with the police statement before presenting the deposition admission)
- [ ] The Notes section does not flag any missing page/line numbers (all are present in this input)
- [ ] The output does not use the words "lied," "perjury," or "fabricated" anywhere
- [ ] Webb's statement that he looked at Maps "just a quick glance" vs. "I wouldn't call that using my phone" is not flagged as a separate contradiction — it is a characterization dispute, not a factual contradiction (this tests the guardrail against opinion/characterization misclassification)

## What failure looks like

A failing output would: paraphrase testimony instead of quoting verbatim; assign the turning-direction internal contradiction to Tier 1 without acknowledging that Webb's explanation (diagram confusion) makes it susceptible to rehabilitation; omit the cross-witness finding between Webb and Finch on phone visibility; flag the "quick glance" / "not really using my phone" exchange as a separate contradiction (it is not — it is a characterization dispute on the same conceded fact); or provide citations that do not match the page:line numbers in the input (e.g., citing Dep. 93 as "p. 93, line 1" in inconsistent format is tolerable, but omitting citations entirely is a fail).
