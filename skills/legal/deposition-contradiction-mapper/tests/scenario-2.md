# Scenario 2: Commercial Contract Dispute — Single Witness, Three-Way Inconsistency on Notice Timing

## Context

A litigation associate at a defense firm is preparing cross-examination outlines for a corporate representative deposition in *Meridian Systems Corp. v. Okafor Industrial Partners LLC*, a commercial contract dispute. The plaintiff alleges defendant breached a software licensing agreement and knew of the breach but failed to cure within the contractual notice-and-cure period. The defendant's corporate representative, Sandra Okafor (Chief Operating Officer), was deposed for a full day with a morning and afternoon session. Okafor also provided written interrogatory answers, signed under penalty of perjury, six months before the deposition. The material question for trial is exactly when Okafor first became aware of the alleged breach — because the contract's cure period began running from that date, and the plaintiff argues the cure period lapsed. There is no second witness. The associate needs the contradiction brief to build an impeachment sequence targeting Okafor's account of when she first learned of the breach.

## Input

---

### CASE SUMMARY

*Meridian Systems Corp. v. Okafor Industrial Partners LLC*, Case No. 2024-CV-03314. Plaintiff Meridian alleges defendant Okafor Industrial Partners breached Section 7.2 of their enterprise software licensing agreement by deploying Meridian's software on unlicensed server instances beginning in Q3 2024. The contract provides a 30-day cure period running from the date defendant's authorized representative first becomes aware of the breach. Plaintiff contends the cure period began on or before September 15, 2024 (the date of a documented internal audit report circulated to Okafor's team) and lapsed before defendant took corrective action. The central disputed fact is when Sandra Okafor, as COO and authorized representative, first had actual knowledge of the breach.

### DEPOSITION OF SANDRA OKAFOR (Corporate Representative, Okafor Industrial Partners LLC), JANUARY 9, 2025 (Excerpts)

**MORNING SESSION — Pages 1–98**

Okafor Dep. 34:7–14
Q: Ms. Okafor, when did you first become personally aware that the company's server deployment may have exceeded the licensed instance count under the Meridian agreement?
A: It was in late September. I believe it was late September of last year — 2024. Someone from the IT compliance team flagged it to me.

Okafor Dep. 34:15–22
Q: Can you be more specific than "late September"?
A: Late September is as specific as I can be. The last week or two of September. I don't recall the exact date.

Okafor Dep. 35:1–8
Q: Did you receive any written communication about this at the time?
A: I'm sure there were emails. I don't have them in front of me. The IT team would have sent something to my team.

Okafor Dep. 35:9–16
Q: Do you recall receiving a copy of the Q3 internal audit report prepared by your IT compliance team?
A: I may have. There are a lot of reports that come through. I can't confirm I personally read that particular one.

Okafor Dep. 52:3–12
Q: Were you involved in any discussions about the Meridian licensing exposure in September 2024?
A: Not in September, no — not that I can point to a specific conversation. It became more of a focus in October, after the board meeting.

Okafor Dep. 60:14–21
Q: The board meeting you just referenced — which board meeting was that?
A: Our Q3 board meeting. We present compliance items on the agenda. That's usually when things that need executive attention get formally flagged.

**AFTERNOON SESSION — Pages 99–184**

Okafor Dep. 128:5–16
Q: Ms. Okafor, I want to come back to the timing of when you first learned about the Meridian licensing issue. You testified this morning that it was late September. Is that still your testimony?
A: I said late September, but I want to clarify. When I think about it more carefully, I believe I was first made formally aware of it after our Q3 board meeting. Which would have been mid-October.

Okafor Dep. 128:17–22
Q: So you're revising your answer from late September to mid-October?
A: Not revising — clarifying. The issue may have been floating around in late September at the staff level, but I didn't have formal awareness until after the board meeting.

Okafor Dep. 129:1–9
Q: What is the difference between "floating around at the staff level" and your being aware of it?
A: For something to be on my radar in a formal sense, it needs to come to me through a formal channel. An email to my team is not the same as a briefing to me personally.

Okafor Dep. 129:10–18
Q: To be clear: your testimony is that you first became aware of the Meridian licensing issue after the Q3 board meeting in mid-October?
A: That's my best recollection, yes. Mid-October, after the board meeting.

Okafor Dep. 141:2–9
Q: What is the approximate date of the Q3 board meeting?
A: Our Q3 board meetings are typically held in the second week of October. I would say October 12 or 13 of last year, approximately.

Okafor Dep. 141:10–16
Q: And your testimony is that you did not have awareness of the breach prior to that meeting?
A: In a formal sense, no. Not prior to the board meeting.

### PRIOR SWORN STATEMENT — OKAFOR INTERROGATORY ANSWERS, PLAINTIFF'S FIRST SET, ANSWER NO. 7

*Case No. 2024-CV-03314, Meridian Systems Corp. v. Okafor Industrial Partners LLC*
*Defendant's Answers and Objections to Plaintiff's First Set of Interrogatories*
*Signed under penalty of perjury by Sandra Okafor, COO, November 2, 2024*

**Interrogatory No. 7**: State the date on which any officer, director, or authorized representative of Defendant first became aware, or should have become aware, that Defendant's server deployment exceeded the licensed instance count under the Meridian licensing agreement.

**Answer No. 7**: Subject to and without waiving the foregoing general objections, Defendant states: Based on a review of available records, an authorized representative of Defendant first became aware of a potential discrepancy in server instance counts no later than September 15, 2024. This date reflects the circulation date of Defendant's Q3 internal IT compliance audit report, which was distributed to personnel with authority to take corrective action.

## Expected Output Criteria

- [ ] The brief header correctly lists Sandra Okafor as the sole witness and Interrogatory Answer No. 7 (signed November 2, 2024) as the prior sworn statement analyzed
- [ ] The brief explicitly states "No cross-witness contradictions exist — only one witness analyzed" or equivalent language in the appropriate section, and does not leave the cross-witness section blank or simply absent
- [ ] The brief identifies the morning testimony (late September) and afternoon testimony (mid-October, after the Q3 board meeting) as an INTERNAL contradiction, placed at Tier 1, with verbatim quotes from both sessions and specific page:line citations for both
- [ ] The Tier 1 internal contradiction ranking is justified on materiality grounds — the brief's cross-examination note or tier rationale makes clear that the contradiction goes directly to the notice-timing issue that determines whether the cure period lapsed
- [ ] The brief identifies a PRIOR SWORN contradiction between Okafor's deposition testimony (mid-October per Dep. 128:5–129:18) and Interrogatory Answer No. 7 ("no later than September 15, 2024"), placed at Tier 1, with verbatim text from both the deposition and the interrogatory answer
- [ ] The brief identifies the three-way nature of the inconsistency — that Okafor gave three distinct accounts (September 15 in the interrogatory answer, "late September" in the morning deposition, "mid-October after the board meeting" in the afternoon deposition) — whether as a single complex entry or as related entries that are cross-referenced
- [ ] The interrogatory answer is correctly identified as a prior sworn statement (signed under penalty of perjury), not merely a discovery response
- [ ] The cross-examination note for the key Tier 1 item addresses deployment sequence — specifically, how to use the interrogatory answer against the afternoon testimony (which is the most favorable account for plaintiff and most damaging to defendant)
- [ ] Okafor's afternoon hedging ("not revising — clarifying," "formal awareness" vs. staff-level awareness) is flagged either as a susceptibility-to-rehabilitation factor (affecting tier reasoning) or in the Notes section as a known defense argument — it must not be ignored
- [ ] The output correctly does not invent a cross-witness contradiction by treating the interrogatory answer as a "second witness" — the interrogatory answer is Okafor's own prior sworn statement, and the contradiction type should be PRIOR SWORN, not CROSS-WITNESS

## What failure looks like

A failing output would: treat the interrogatory answer as if it were from a second witness and label the finding CROSS-WITNESS; fail to flag the three-way inconsistency across all three accounts (September 15, late September, mid-October); place the internal contradiction at Tier 2 without explaining why it is not Tier 1 given that notice timing is the central material issue; omit or silently skip the cross-witness section without explicitly noting that no cross-witness contradictions exist; or quote Okafor's testimony in paraphrase rather than verbatim. A borderline output would identify only two of the three accounts (e.g., morning vs. interrogatory answer, ignoring the afternoon pivot) — this is a fail because it misses the most strategically significant inconsistency (the afternoon "mid-October" answer is the one that, if believed, would benefit defendant most, yet it conflicts with defendant's own interrogatory answer).
