# Scenario 2: UK Employment Rights Act 2025 (Worker Status / Gig Economy) + California FAST Recovery Act Wage Order — Gig Delivery Platform

## Context

A compliance attorney at a national law firm is advising Quickhop Delivery Group Ltd., a gig-economy delivery platform that operates in the United Kingdom and California. Quickhop's business model: couriers sign up as independent contractors, accept delivery jobs via Quickhop's app, and are paid per completed delivery. Quickhop has approximately 11,000 couriers in the UK and 4,800 in California. Two regulatory instruments affecting gig-platform worker classification and wage obligations landed simultaneously: the UK Employment Rights Act 2025 (ERA 2025), which contains new provisions on worker status for platform workers, and the California FAST Recovery Act Wage Order setting minimum wage and sector-specific obligations for fast-food and delivery platform workers. The attorney needs to produce a single client memo covering both, with a clear action checklist distinguishing the differing deadlines and obligation levels in each jurisdiction.

## Input

---

### Instrument 1 — United Kingdom: Employment Rights Act 2025 (ERA 2025) — Platform Worker Provisions (Phased Commencement)

**Issuing authority**: UK Parliament / Department for Business and Trade
**Royal Assent**: 26 October 2025
**Commencement**: Provisions on platform worker information rights commenced 6 April 2026. Provisions on guaranteed hours and statutory sick pay for zero-hours and "low hours" platform workers commence 6 April 2027 (subject to secondary legislation confirming the exact start date). Provisions on unfair dismissal reform (removal of two-year qualifying period for unfair dismissal claims) commence 6 April 2026.

**Section 1 — Definitions (Platform Worker):**
A "platform worker" is defined as a worker who provides personal services to a platform operator primarily through a digital platform and whose work allocation, pricing, and rating is substantially controlled by the platform's algorithmic systems. A "platform operator" is any business that deploys a digital platform to allocate work to platform workers. The ERA 2025 does not alter the pre-existing three-category legal distinction between "employees," "workers," and "self-employed contractors" — platform workers who meet the statutory "worker" definition under the Employment Rights Act 1996 are already covered by worker protections; Section 1 creates a new sub-category ("platform workers") with additional specific rights layered on top of the worker classification.

**Section 14 — Right to Information (commenced 6 April 2026):**
Platform operators must provide each platform worker, before the worker's first work allocation, with a written statement of: (a) the algorithmic criteria used to allocate work to the worker; (b) the performance metrics and ratings that determine the worker's access to work; (c) the circumstances in which the platform operator may restrict or terminate the worker's access to the platform. The statement must be updated within 14 days of any material change to these criteria. Failure to provide the statement is actionable in the Employment Tribunal; compensation is set at a minimum of two weeks' pay up to the statutory cap.

**Section 21 — Guaranteed Hours Obligation (commences 6 April 2027 — subject to secondary legislation):**
Platform operators must offer platform workers who have worked for them for 12 consecutive weeks at an average of 10 or more hours per week a guaranteed hours contract reflecting their average hours worked over the preceding 12-week reference period, rounded to the nearest whole hour. Operators are not required to guarantee hours above the 12-week average. Workers may decline the guaranteed hours offer without losing their current access to the platform. Operators may apply to the Secretary of State for an exemption if the nature of the platform makes guaranteed hours offers operationally impractical — the exemption criteria and application process are to be set by secondary legislation not yet published.

**Section 22 — Statutory Sick Pay for Platform Workers (commences 6 April 2027):**
Platform workers whose earnings from the platform in the eight weeks prior to illness meet the lower earnings limit (£123 per week for the 2026/27 tax year) are entitled to Statutory Sick Pay (SSP) for qualifying days of sickness. Platform operators must implement a mechanism for workers to report sickness and trigger SSP eligibility. The reporting and payment mechanism must be established by 6 April 2027.

**Section 37 — Unfair Dismissal Reform (commenced 6 April 2026):**
The two-year qualifying period for unfair dismissal claims is removed for "employees" (not "workers" or "platform workers") as of 6 April 2026. This provision applies to employees, not to platform workers in the sub-category defined under Section 1 — however, ERA 2025 also amends the Employment Rights Act 1996 to clarify that deactivation of a platform worker's account by a platform operator may constitute constructive dismissal if the platform worker meets the threshold to be classified as an "employee" under existing case law.

**Competent authority**: Employment Tribunals (individual claims); Employment Agency Standards Inspectorate (proactive enforcement of information rights under Section 14).

**Safe harbor / Exemption**: None for Section 14. The Section 21 guaranteed hours exemption process is set by forthcoming secondary legislation.

### Instrument 2 — California, United States: FAST Recovery Act Wage Order — Delivery Platform Sector Addendum (Effective 1 September 2025)

**Issuing authority**: California Fast Food Council / California Department of Industrial Relations
**Effective date**: 1 September 2025

**Scope**: Applies to "covered delivery platforms," defined as any business that: (a) operates a digital platform through which food or consumer goods delivery services are offered to the public; (b) uses independent contractor or gig-worker couriers to fulfill deliveries; (c) operates in California with 100 or more couriers performing deliveries in the state at any time in the preceding calendar quarter.

**Minimum compensation floor:**
Covered delivery platforms must ensure that couriers earn a minimum effective hourly rate of $22.00 per hour for all time during which the courier is "engaged" with the platform. "Engaged time" means: time from when the courier accepts a delivery assignment until the delivery is completed or cancelled, plus any documented waiting time at a merchant pickup location exceeding five minutes. Waiting time at home or standby time between assignments is explicitly excluded from engaged time. Platforms must implement a tracking and true-up mechanism: if a courier's total earnings in a calendar month divided by total engaged minutes fall below the $22.00/hour floor, the platform must pay the shortfall ("true-up payment") by the 15th of the following month.

**Transparency obligations:**
Covered delivery platforms must provide each courier with a monthly earnings statement showing: total assignments accepted, total engaged time (in minutes), total earnings before any platform fees or deductions, total platform fees or deductions itemized, and net earnings. The statement must identify whether any true-up payment was made and the calculation basis.

**Benefits access obligation:**
Covered delivery platforms must contribute $1.50 per engaged hour to a portable benefits account designated by each courier. Contributions must be remitted monthly. Acceptable portable benefits structures include: (a) a state-approved individual benefits account; (b) direct payment to a benefits entity or union designated by the courier; (c) if the courier does not designate a recipient within 30 days of first assignment, contributions must be held in escrow and transferred to the California Portable Benefits Authority.

**Anti-retaliation provision:**
A platform operator may not deactivate or restrict a courier's account in retaliation for asserting rights under this Wage Order. A rebuttable presumption of retaliation arises if deactivation occurs within 90 days of a courier asserting a wage claim or making an inquiry about the wage floor.

**Exemption**: Couriers who are classified as employees (not independent contractors) are covered by the California minimum wage law instead of this Wage Order and are excluded from this Wage Order's scope.

**Competent authority**: California Department of Industrial Relations (DIR); California Labor Commissioner for individual claims.

**Penalty framework**: Failure to pay the minimum compensation floor: civil penalty of $100 per affected courier per pay period. Failure to make portable benefits contributions: penalty of 20% of unpaid contributions per month. Failure to provide monthly earnings statement: $250 per courier per month.

### Client Profile

**Client name**: Quickhop Delivery Group Ltd.
**Business description**: Quickhop operates a gig delivery platform connecting consumers and restaurants/retailers with independent-contractor couriers via a mobile app. Couriers are classified as independent contractors in both the UK and California. Quickhop's app uses an algorithmic dispatch system (DISPATCH-AI) that assigns jobs based on courier proximity, acceptance rate history, and customer ratings. In the UK, approximately 11,000 couriers regularly engage with the platform; in California, approximately 4,800 couriers. Couriers are not employed by Quickhop — they are self-employed and control their own working hours, though Quickhop's dispatch algorithm controls job allocation and ratings affect access to premium assignments. In California, Quickhop processed an average of 5,200 active couriers per quarter in 2025. Average UK courier earnings are approximately £15.40/hour during engaged delivery time; average California courier earnings are approximately $19.80/hour during engaged delivery time. Quickhop does not currently provide any written algorithm disclosure, earnings statements beyond a payment summary, or portable benefits contributions.
**Geographic footprint**: United Kingdom; California, United States
**Entity characteristics**: UK-incorporated (private limited company). Parent entity is Quickhop Holdings BV, incorporated in the Netherlands. Approximately 320 employees (non-courier staff). Couriers are classified as independent contractors in both jurisdictions. No UK employee meets the Section 1 ERA 2025 "platform worker" definition — only couriers are platform workers.
**Key contracts or policies**: (1) Standard Courier Agreement (UK version) — designates couriers as independent contractors; does not include algorithm disclosure or guaranteed hours provisions. (2) Standard Courier Agreement (California version) — designates couriers as independent contractors; does not include wage floor true-up mechanism or portable benefits provisions. (3) DISPATCH-AI algorithm documentation — internal only, not currently shared with couriers.
**Role titles**: Chief Executive Officer, Head of UK Operations, Head of US Operations, General Counsel, Chief Product Officer (responsible for DISPATCH-AI), Head of Finance
**Memo date**: 2026-06-03
