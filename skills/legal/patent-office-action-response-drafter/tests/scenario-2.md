# Scenario 2: Mixed Office Action — §102 Anticipation and §112 Written Description on a Mechanical/Device Claim

## Context

A patent agent at a solo practice represents an independent inventor in Application No. 17/503,887, titled "Adjustable-Torque Fastener Assembly with Overload-Release Mechanism for Structural Connections." The application is directed to a mechanical device — a fastener assembly used in structural steel connections (bridges, building frames) that incorporates a calibrated torque-release mechanism to prevent bolt overloading. Art Unit is 3663 (Transportation, Construction, Electronic Commerce). A Non-Final Office Action was mailed November 18, 2025, with a response deadline of February 18, 2026.

The examiner has issued two separate rejections: (1) claims 1, 3, and 6 rejected under 35 U.S.C. §102(a)(1) as anticipated by Hernandez (US 8,943,912 B2, "Torque-Limiting Fastener Assembly for Mechanical Joints"); and (2) claim 8 rejected under 35 U.S.C. §112(a) for lack of written description support.

The patent agent needs to prepare a full response addressing both rejection types. She has the Office Action, the pending claims, and the Hernandez reference.

## Input

### PENDING CLAIMS

**Claim 1** (Independent): A fastener assembly for a structural steel connection, comprising:
a bolt having a shank, a threaded distal end, and a proximal head;
a calibrated torque-release collar circumferentially surrounding the bolt shank proximate to the proximal head, the collar comprising:
  (i) a shear ring formed from a material having a predefined shear strength, the shear ring being concentrically seated between the collar body and the bolt shank,
  (ii) a visual indicator element disposed on an outer surface of the collar, the visual indicator element being configured to produce a visible color change when the shear ring fractures, and
  (iii) a plurality of circumferentially distributed retention tabs extending radially inward from the collar body and engaging a corresponding annular groove formed in the bolt shank, wherein the retention tabs are configured to disengage from the annular groove upon shear ring fracture;
a load-distribution washer disposed between the proximal bolt head and a bearing surface of the structural connection, the load-distribution washer having a diameter at least 1.5 times the diameter of the bolt shank; and
wherein fracture of the shear ring produces a tactile pulse detectable by a hand-tool operator applying torque to the proximal bolt head.

**Claim 3** (Dependent on Claim 1): The fastener assembly of claim 1, wherein the visual indicator element comprises a sealed capsule containing a chromogenic dye, the capsule being positioned within a recess formed in the outer surface of the collar body such that fracture of the shear ring transmits a compressive force through the collar body sufficient to rupture the capsule and release the dye to the outer surface.

**Claim 6** (Dependent on Claim 1): The fastener assembly of claim 1, wherein the load-distribution washer comprises a hardened steel plate with a Rockwell hardness of at least HRC 35, and wherein the washer includes a plurality of radially oriented score lines on its bearing surface to increase friction engagement with the structural connection surface.

**Claim 8** (Dependent on Claim 1): The fastener assembly of claim 1, wherein the shear ring material has a temperature coefficient of shear strength between -0.15% per degree Celsius and -0.25% per degree Celsius across an operating temperature range of -40°C to 120°C, such that the release torque threshold varies by no more than ±12% across the operating temperature range.

### EXAMINER'S REJECTIONS (from Office Action, mailed November 18, 2025)

**Rejection 1 — 35 U.S.C. §102(a)(1): Claims 1, 3, and 6 — Anticipated by Hernandez**

Claims 1, 3, and 6 are rejected under 35 U.S.C. §102(a)(1) as being anticipated by Hernandez (US 8,943,912 B2).

Hernandez discloses a fastener assembly for mechanical joint connections (Hernandez, Abstract), comprising a bolt with shank, threaded distal end, and proximal head (col. 2, ll. 10–22), and a torque-limiting collar surrounding the bolt shank (col. 3, ll. 1–40). The Hernandez collar includes a sacrificial fracture element formed from a calibrated material disposed between the collar and bolt shank (col. 3, ll. 41–60), which is substantially similar to the claimed shear ring. Hernandez further discloses retention elements (referred to as "locking tabs") extending inward from the collar body to engage a groove in the bolt shank (col. 4, ll. 12–28). Hernandez discloses a color indicator strip on the collar outer surface that changes color upon fracture (col. 5, ll. 1–15), and discloses a load distribution washer positioned between the bolt head and connection surface with a diameter described as "substantially greater than the bolt diameter" (col. 6, ll. 22–35). Regarding claim 3's chromogenic dye capsule, Hernandez discloses a "sealed indicator module containing a reactive dye compound" positioned in the collar body (col. 5, ll. 8–22). Regarding claim 6, Hernandez discloses a hardened washer with surface texturing described as "friction-enhancing surface features" (col. 6, ll. 30–42).

The examiner finds that Hernandez discloses each and every limitation of independent claim 1 and dependent claims 3 and 6, and therefore these claims are anticipated.

**Rejection 2 — 35 U.S.C. §112(a): Claim 8 — Lack of Written Description**

Claim 8 is rejected under 35 U.S.C. §112(a) as failing to comply with the written description requirement. The claimed limitation recites a specific temperature coefficient of shear strength between -0.15% per degree Celsius and -0.25% per degree Celsius across a temperature range of -40°C to 120°C, with a resulting release-torque variation of no more than ±12% across that range. The specification, as filed, does not describe any shear ring material with these specific temperature-dependent properties. The specification at paragraph [0045] generally states that "the shear ring may be formed from materials selected for temperature stability," and at paragraph [0047] notes that "suitable shear ring materials include certain aluminum alloys, brass alloys, and engineering thermoplastics," but does not identify any specific material, provide any test data, or describe the temperature coefficient range recited in claim 8. The examiner finds that the specification does not demonstrate that the inventors possessed shear ring materials with the specific quantitative temperature-response properties recited in the claim at the time of filing.

### CITED REFERENCE EXCERPTS

**Hernandez (US 8,943,912 B2) — Relevant Portions**

Abstract: "A torque-limiting collar assembly for use with standard hex-head fasteners in industrial and mechanical joint applications, providing a visual indication of fastener pre-load achievement."

Col. 2, ll. 10–22: "The fastener assembly 10 comprises a hex-head bolt 12 having a smooth cylindrical shank 14, threaded end portion 16, and hexagonal head 18. The collar assembly 20 is fitted over the shank 14 adjacent to the bolt head 18."

Col. 3, ll. 1–40: "The collar body 22 circumferentially surrounds the bolt shank 14. An internal fracture element 24, formed from a material calibrated to fracture at a predetermined torque level, is disposed between the collar body inner bore and the shank outer surface. The fracture element 24 is concentrically positioned and retained by the collar body geometry."

Col. 3, ll. 41–60: "The fracture element 24 is an annular ring of predetermined cross-sectional area. Selection of ring material and cross-section determines the torque threshold at which fracture occurs. Suitable materials include zinc alloys and certain plastics, selected for consistent fracture characteristics."

Col. 4, ll. 12–28: "A set of inward-extending locking tabs 26 are formed on the inner bore of the collar body 22. The locking tabs 26 engage a circumferential groove 28 formed on the bolt shank 14 to retain the collar assembly 20 in position during installation. Upon fracture of the fracture element 24, the locking tabs 26 release from groove 28, permitting relative rotation between the collar 22 and bolt 12."

Col. 4, ll. 45–55: "The collar assembly 20 is designed for use with industrial bolts in the M16 through M30 diameter range, for non-structural mechanical joints in manufacturing and equipment assembly applications. The assembly is not rated for use in primary structural connections in buildings or bridges."

Col. 5, ll. 1–22: "A color indicator strip 30 is bonded to the outer surface of the collar body 22. The indicator strip 30 is formed from a thermochromic coating that changes color when the collar body 22 is subjected to the torsional stress of fracture element failure. In an alternative embodiment, a sealed indicator module 32 containing a reactive dye compound is positioned within a recess in the collar body 22; fracture-induced deformation ruptures the module 32 and releases dye to the collar outer surface."

Col. 6, ll. 22–42: "A flat washer 34 is positioned between the bolt head 18 and the joint surface. The washer 34 has a diameter substantially greater than the bolt shank diameter to distribute bearing load. The washer 34 may include surface features on its bearing face to increase friction engagement with the joint surface."

Col. 6, ll. 43–55: "The collar assembly of this invention is intended for torque-indicating purposes in non-critical mechanical joints. Suitability for structural engineering applications, including bridge and building frame connections subject to seismic or dynamic loading, has not been evaluated and is not claimed."

## Expected Output Criteria

- [ ] The skill produces a Rejection Inventory table correctly identifying both rejection grounds: (1) §102(a)(1) anticipation by Hernandez covering claims 1, 3, and 6; and (2) §112(a) written description deficiency covering claim 8.
- [ ] For the §102 anticipation rejection, the limitation-by-limitation analysis identifies at minimum one unambiguous distinction between claim 1 and Hernandez: claim 1 requires the shear ring to produce "a tactile pulse detectable by a hand-tool operator" upon fracture — Hernandez discloses color indication and locking tab release but contains no disclosure of a tactile/haptic signal to the operator; the skill must identify and develop this gap specifically.
- [ ] The §102 argument section addresses the scope limitation in Hernandez col. 4, ll. 45–55 and col. 6, ll. 43–55: Hernandez explicitly states the assembly is rated for non-structural mechanical joints in manufacturing applications and that suitability for structural engineering applications "has not been evaluated and is not claimed" — the skill should raise this as a scope distinction reinforcing that Hernandez is not directed to structural steel connections as claimed.
- [ ] For the §112(a) written description rejection on claim 8, the skill identifies the specific deficiency: the specification's general reference to "temperature stability" at paragraph [0045] and generic material list at paragraph [0047] do not identify any specific material with the quantitative temperature-coefficient properties recited in claim 8, and the response must acknowledge this gap honestly.
- [ ] The §112 response section proposes a targeted remedy: either a substantive argument (if specification support exists that was overlooked by the examiner) or a proposed claim amendment narrowing claim 8 to language that the specification does support — and that amendment must be flagged as "[PRACTITIONER AUTHORIZATION REQUIRED]" with the estoppel consequence noted.
- [ ] The Practitioner Review Checklist addresses both rejection types and flags: (a) the §102 claim amendment option (e.g., adding "tactile pulse" or "structural steel connection" language to independent claim 1 if needed); (b) the §112 open question of whether any test data or declaration evidence can be submitted under 37 C.F.R. § 1.132 to establish written description support for the temperature coefficient range; (c) the response deadline verification.

## What failure looks like

A failing output would: (a) fail to identify the "tactile pulse" limitation as the clearest §102 distinction — the most obvious argument hook in the claim — and instead argue only generally that Hernandez is "different"; (b) miss the Hernandez disclaimer of structural application scope (col. 4 and col. 6), which distinguishes the application environment of the claims; (c) for the §112 rejection, either falsely assert that the specification provides written description support it does not actually provide, or simply restate the examiner's rejection without proposing a cure; (d) omit the proposed §112 remedy (amendment or declaration) from the Practitioner Review Checklist; (e) present the §112 amendment as finalized rather than flagged for authorization; or (f) address the two rejection types in a merged or disorganized section rather than clearly separated §102 and §112 argument sections.
