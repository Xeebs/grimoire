# Scenario 1: Regional Bank Losing Customers to Digital-First Competitors

## Context

A senior associate at a mid-tier strategy firm is building a competitive benchmarking slide for a client deck. The client is Heartland Community Bank, a $9B-asset regional bank headquartered in Columbus, Ohio. The partner has framed the core client question as: "Where are we losing profitable retail customers to digital-first competitors?"

The associate has spent two days pulling competitor data from J.D. Power reports, annual reports, investor day presentations, and an industry database (Bankwell Analytics). They have raw figures for four competitors — two large national banks and two fintechs — across six metrics. Several complications have surfaced:
- One fintech (Crest Digital) does not report a "digital adoption rate" — it reports "monthly active users" as a raw count, not as a percentage of its customer base
- The NPS figure for Meridian National Bank is from a J.D. Power study published 22 months ago (January 2023, memo date November 2024)
- The customer acquisition cost (CAC) figure for Continental US Bank is not from its annual report — it was estimated in a Bankwell Analytics industry report
- The associate wants to highlight NIM and NPS gaps because they are numerically large, but the partner's stated question is specifically about customer loss to digital-first players

The associate invokes the skill to normalize the data, build the comparison table, and identify the gaps that actually matter for the client question.

## Expected Output Criteria

- [ ] The comparison table includes all six metrics as rows, all four competitors plus Heartland as columns, and a Peer Median column — with source tags and data type markers ([R] or [E]) in each cell
- [ ] Meridian's NPS cell is marked [STALE] because the January 2023 J.D. Power data is 22 months old relative to the November 2024 memo date
- [ ] Continental's CAC cell is marked [E] because the value comes from a Bankwell Analytics estimate, not a company filing or press release
- [ ] Both fintech digital adoption cells (Crest Digital and Apex Neobank) are marked [NC] with a note directing to Definition Notes, because they report MAU counts, not a penetration rate
- [ ] The Peer Median for Digital Adoption Rate is calculated from reported values only (Meridian and Continental) and excludes the two fintechs — OR is marked [Insufficient data] with an explanation
- [ ] Definition Notes include an entry for Digital Adoption Rate that documents the MAU vs. penetration-rate mismatch and explicitly states the figures are not comparable
- [ ] Definition Notes include an entry for Net Interest Margin noting Crest Digital's non-GAAP definition (includes interchange-equivalent revenue) vs. Heartland's GAAP NIM, and flags the incomparability
- [ ] Strategic Gap Analysis selects CAC and Digital Adoption Rate as two of the primary gaps — these are the tier-1 metrics for the stated customer-loss question — rather than defaulting to the largest raw numerical variances
- [ ] The NIM gap is either excluded from the Strategic Gap Analysis or noted as contextually secondary, because NIM is not directly relevant to the question of losing customers to digital-first competitors
- [ ] The narrative headline is a specific, falsifiable claim tied to the digital-first customer loss question — not a generic "Heartland faces competitive pressure" statement
- [ ] Each callout box includes a data quality note where applicable (e.g., CAC figures for fintechs are estimates)
- [ ] The Narrative Arc addresses how to handle the CAC data quality pushback in the client meeting

## What failure looks like

A failing output: (a) treats Crest Digital and Apex Neobank's MAU figures as comparable to Heartland's digital adoption percentage and computes a variance anyway; (b) does not flag Meridian's NPS as stale; (c) marks Continental's CAC as [R] because the model did not read the source type carefully; (d) selects NIM as a strategic gap because the Crest Digital-vs.-Heartland gap looks large, without noting the incomparable definition; (e) writes a headline like "Heartland lags digital competitors on key metrics" — which is non-falsifiable and strategically empty; (f) omits Definition Notes entirely or writes them as a one-line mention rather than a structured entry per metric.
