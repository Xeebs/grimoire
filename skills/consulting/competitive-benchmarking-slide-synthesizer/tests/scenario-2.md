# Scenario 2: B2B SaaS Client Evaluating Pricing and Positioning Against Enterprise Competitors

## Context

A business analyst at a consulting firm is building a competitive benchmarking slide for a client deck. The client is Vaultify, a B2B SaaS company selling enterprise document workflow automation software. Vaultify competes for large enterprise deals (ACV $150K+) primarily against three players: Formidable (a public US company), Arcana Systems (a public European company reporting in EUR), and Nexus Workflow (a private company that went public via S-1 two years ago and has since been acquired; the S-1 is the only detailed public disclosure available).

The partner's strategic question is: "Are we priced and positioned to win enterprise deals against our top three competitors?"

The analyst has compiled the following raw data. Key complications:
- Formidable reports ARR in USD; Arcana Systems reports ARR in EUR — the analyst has noted the EUR/USD rate at time of Arcana's most recent report (0.92) and the current spot rate (0.89), but has not yet applied a conversion
- Nexus Workflow's net revenue retention (NRR) figure comes from its S-1 filed 26 months ago (September 2022, memo date November 2024) — this is the only NRR figure publicly available
- Win rate data for all three competitors is estimated from a Gartner Peer Insights analyst note, not from company disclosures; Vaultify's win rate is from its internal CRM pipeline data (reported)
- R&D spend as a % of revenue for Arcana is stated in its annual report, but the report notes that Arcana classifies "product localization costs" as R&D — a definition that most US SaaS companies would classify as COGS or S&M

The analyst needs to determine which gaps are most relevant to the pricing and positioning question and which gaps to surface in the client deck.

## Expected Output Criteria

- [ ] The comparison table includes all seven metrics as rows and all three competitors plus Vaultify as columns, with a Peer Median column, source tags, and [R]/[E] markers in each cell
- [ ] Arcana's ARR and ACV cells include a currency conversion note — either the value is converted to USD with the conversion rate documented in Definition Notes, or the cells are flagged [NC] with an explicit note that direct comparison requires currency normalization
- [ ] All Nexus Workflow cells are marked [STALE] because the S-1 data is 26 months old relative to the November 2024 memo date
- [ ] Win rate cells for Formidable, Arcana, and Nexus are marked [E] because they come from a Gartner estimate; Vaultify's win rate cell is marked [R] because it comes from internal CRM data
- [ ] Definition Notes include an entry for Gross Margin documenting that Arcana reports on a total revenue basis (including professional services) while Formidable and Vaultify report on subscription revenue only — and notes whether this makes Arcana's figure directly comparable or not
- [ ] Definition Notes include an entry for R&D Spend noting Arcana's inclusion of product localization costs, which most US SaaS peers classify as COGS or S&M, and notes the directional impact on comparability (Arcana's R&D % is likely overstated relative to peers)
- [ ] Strategic Gap Analysis selects ACV, NRR, and/or win rate as the primary strategic gaps — because the client question is about pricing (ACV) and positioning to win enterprise deals (win rate, NRR as a proxy for customer stickiness and expansion) — rather than defaulting to ARR size gap or R&D spend
- [ ] The NRR gap analysis acknowledges that Nexus's NRR is stale and that the gap between Vaultify (104%) and Formidable (118%) is the more reliable and more strategically relevant comparison
- [ ] The narrative headline specifically addresses pricing and/or positioning relative to enterprise deal outcomes — not a generic SaaS competitiveness statement
- [ ] The narrative arc anticipates the objection that Nexus Workflow data is too old to be reliable and advises how to handle it in the client meeting

## What failure looks like

A failing output: (a) compares Arcana's EUR ARR directly to Vaultify's USD ARR without any normalization note or conversion, treating €284M and $89M as directly comparable in a peer median calculation; (b) does not flag any Nexus Workflow data as stale; (c) marks Formidable and Arcana win rates as [R] because the model did not identify the Gartner source as an estimate; (d) selects ARR size gap as a strategic gap without noting that absolute ARR scale is not directly relevant to the question of whether Vaultify is priced to win individual enterprise deals; (e) ignores the Arcana R&D definition issue entirely in Definition Notes; (f) writes a headline focused on ARR scale disadvantage rather than ACV positioning or win rate dynamics; (g) includes Nexus Workflow in the Peer Median for NRR without acknowledging that a 26-month-old S-1 figure may not reflect the company's current state.
